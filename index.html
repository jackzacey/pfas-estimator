Clear next steps

Use the result to make a practical water plan

1. Confirm your water system. Look for the utility name on your water bill. A ZIP code can include several systems, so this is the most important first step.

2. Read the PFAS comparison. The site shows the highest yearly average reported across each system’s EPA sampling locations. “At or above” is a signal to investigate further; it is not proof that every home has the same level. “Below” is not a guarantee that a home is PFAS-free.

3. If you want a filter, verify the exact certification. EPA recommends checking the product label and an accredited certification directory for an NSF/ANSI 53 or NSF/ANSI 58 PFAS-reduction claim. Replace filters on schedule. Current certifications do not necessarily show that a device reduces PFAS to every 2024 EPA limit.
