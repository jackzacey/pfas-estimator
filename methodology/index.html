Methodology & data provenance

How PFAS Estimator converts EPA UCMR 5 public drinking-water monitoring records into searchable ZIP-code results, descriptive national statistics, and carefully bounded clinical context.

The central distinction

PFAS Estimator reports observed public-water monitoring results; it does not model contamination. It does not estimate a household's dose, diagnose disease, assign personal health risk, or determine whether a water system is legally in or out of compliance.

14,071 ZIP codes with at least one retained detection
40,311 compound–ZIP records
4,885 public water systems represented
12 analytes in the processed dataset

PFAS Estimator has two linked components: a national descriptive analysis of EPA monitoring records and a compound-specific clinical translation framework. The monitoring measurements and threshold classifications are deterministic. On-demand language-model summaries sit on top of those fixed records and do not change the underlying concentration, water-system name, or threshold status.

01

Data source & analytic scope

Federal occurrence monitoring, preserved as a versioned project snapshot.

The source is the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's Fifth Unregulated Contaminant Monitoring Rule (UCMR 5) occurrence dataset. UCMR 5 collected samples from 2023 through 2025 for 29 PFAS and lithium. Monitoring covered community and non-transient non-community public water systems serving 3,300 or more people, plus a nationally representative sample of smaller systems.

The processed PFAS Estimator dataset was last updated in July 2026 and contains retained detections for 11 PFAS plus lithium:

PFOAPFOSPFHxSPFNA HFPO-DAPFBSPFPeAPFBA PFHpA6:2 FTSPFDALithium
Version note. EPA states that the public UCMR 5 release available in August 2026 represents approximately 95% of expected results and that the final release is planned for fall 2026. PFAS Estimator is a snapshot of the source data used for this build; later EPA revisions may change counts or individual records.
02

Processing pipeline

From EPA records to one retained observation per compound and ZIP code.

  1. 1

    Import the EPA monitoring records

    UCMR 5 occurrence records and their public-water-system identifiers are ingested. Concentrations are carried in micrograms per liter (µg/L).

  2. 2

    Link public water systems to ZIP codes

    Records are joined to the ZIP codes served by each system using the Public Water System Identifier (PWSID). When one system serves more than one ZIP code, its monitoring record can be assigned to each associated ZIP.

  3. 3

    Exclude non-detections

    Results below the UCMR 5 minimum reporting level are excluded from the analytic file. Therefore, every represented ZIP code has at least one retained detection, and a ZIP with no result must not be interpreted as contamination-free.

  4. 4

    Retain the highest observation

    If a compound appears more than once for the same ZIP code, the highest observed concentration from 2023–2025 is retained. This produces one record per compound–ZIP pair and characterizes the upper bound of observed measurements, not a long-term average.

  5. 5

    Apply the comparison framework

    Each retained concentration is compared with the compound's applicable federal or evidence-based reference using predefined, compound-specific rules. Comparisons describe the analytic record; they are not regulatory compliance determinations.

  6. 6

    Publish the deterministic record

    The public file stores the compound, observed concentration, and water-system name for each retained ZIP record. ZIP lookup, state summaries, tables, and maps read from this processed dataset.

Regulatory status as of August 7, 2026

The analysis uses the MCLs established in EPA's 2024 PFAS drinking-water rule. In May 2026, EPA proposed rescinding the individual PFHxS, PFNA, and HFPO-DA regulations and the mixture Hazard Index, and separately proposed extending the PFOA and PFOS compliance timeline. Those proposals were not final when this page was reviewed. PFAS Estimator preserves its predefined comparison framework and links to EPA for current regulatory status.

04

Descriptive analysis & quality control

Transparent denominators, fixed summaries, and no inferential claims.

Analysis

  • Data processing and descriptive analyses were performed in Python 3.14.2.
  • ZIP code is the primary analytic unit.
  • The denominator is 14,071 ZIP codes with at least one retained detection.
  • Outputs include detected ZIP count, at/above-threshold count and percentage among detected ZIPs, mean detected concentration, and maximum detected concentration.
  • No hypothesis tests, confidence intervals, population weighting, or causal analyses are used.

Quality-control checks

  • 14,071 ZIP-code keys in the processed dataset.
  • 40,311 compound-level records across 12 analytes.
  • No duplicate compound entries within a ZIP code after aggregation.
  • No zero-value concentrations in the retained detection file.
  • State-page totals reconcile with the national processed dataset, apart from the documented Guam page-generation exclusion.
Non-independence is expected. Because one public water system can serve several ZIP codes, ZIP records are not independent neighborhood-level samples. A water-system observation can legitimately appear in multiple ZIPs.
05

Clinical translation layer

Structured evidence context first; generated language second.

Occurrence data and clinical context are stored as separate layers. Each analyte is paired with structured fields covering its full name, toxicologic mechanisms, reported health effects, relevant clinical or research reference values, evidence notes, biological persistence where supported, and source links. The project uses this framework to translate a monitoring result without changing the underlying measurement.

1Observed concentrationfixed UCMR-derived record

Use of a language model

On-demand summaries use Llama 3.3 70B Versatile through the Groq API and a Cloudflare Worker proxy. Prompts supply the matched compound, observed concentration, predefined comparison value where applicable, and selected structured evidence fields. The model is instructed to stay within PFAS and environmental-health topics, acknowledge uncertainty, avoid diagnosis and alarmism, and defer personal medical decisions to a clinician.

  • Not part of the analysis: the language model does not select records, calculate concentrations, set thresholds, or determine whether a value is at or above a comparison level.
  • Variable output: generated text is produced fresh for each query and can vary. Prompt constraints reduce but do not eliminate error.
  • Fallback: when generation is unavailable, the interface can display stored compound-level information instead.
  • Not medical advice: generated text cannot establish an individual's exposure, diagnosis, prognosis, or treatment need.
06

How to interpret a ZIP-code result

What a result can support—and what it cannot.

A result can tell you

  • A monitored public water system associated with the ZIP had a reported detection.
  • The highest observed concentration retained for that compound–ZIP pair.
  • How that concentration compares with the project's predefined federal or research reference.
  • Which water-system name is attached to the retained record.

A result cannot tell you

  • The concentration at a particular home's tap today.
  • An individual's dose, blood level, cumulative exposure, or disease risk.
  • Whether a water system is legally compliant or in violation.
  • Whether an unrepresented ZIP code or private well is free of PFAS.
07

Known limitations

These constraints should travel with every interpretation of the dataset.

Detection-only denominator

ZIP codes in which every monitored result was below the minimum reporting level are absent. The 14,071-ZIP denominator is not all U.S. ZIP codes and cannot support national population-prevalence claims.

Public-water coverage

UCMR 5 does not comprehensively represent small systems, rural communities, or private wells. No-result areas may be unmonitored, below reporting levels, served outside the mapped system, or outside the dataset.

ZIP linkage

ZIP codes are service-area proxies, not precise utility boundaries. One system may supply multiple ZIPs, and one ZIP may contain more than one water source or service arrangement.

Highest observed value

The retained value is the maximum observed concentration for a compound–ZIP pair during the monitoring period. It is not a running annual average, typical concentration, continuous measurement, or household exposure estimate.

Compliance

UCMR occurrence results do not themselves establish MCL compliance or noncompliance. Formal compliance uses regulatory sampling locations, schedules, and averaging rules that this ZIP-level aggregation does not reproduce.

PFAS scope

The processed dataset contains 11 PFAS plus lithium. It does not characterize the full PFAS class, unmeasured precursors, household products, replacement chemicals, source-water conditions, or treatment performance.

Clinical interpretation

Population-level epidemiology and toxicology cannot be converted directly into an individual's diagnosis or probability of harm. Health effects depend on dose, duration, timing, co-exposures, physiology, and other factors not measured here.

Changing source data and rules

EPA may add, revise, or remove records before the final UCMR 5 release, and federal or state standards may change. State agencies and water utilities remain authoritative for current local guidance.

Project governance & review status

Who built the project, what review has occurred, and what has not yet occurred.

Project ownership

PFAS Estimator is an independent, student-led project created by Jack Zhang, a student at Los Gatos High School in California. The project is not sponsored or endorsed by the school, EPA, a university, a health system, or another institution.

Clinical review

Joshua Tamayo-Sarver, MD, PhD, is providing clinical and epidemiologic review of the developing translation framework and manuscript. That collaboration should not be interpreted as institutional endorsement.

Peer-review status

The project and associated manuscript have not yet completed journal peer review or formal independent validation. A manuscript describing the analysis and clinical translation framework is in preparation.

Current verification

Internal checks confirm the processed record counts, absence of zero-value retained concentrations, uniqueness of compound entries within ZIP codes, and reconciliation of generated state summaries with the national file.
