01

Study question and design

The unit of analysis is the community water system—not the ZIP code.

What the study can and cannot answer

The study evaluates monitoring patterns across public water systems and the aggregate characteristics of their estimated service areas. It does not estimate individual exposure, household dose, disease risk, causal effects, or regulatory compliance.

The primary question is whether UCMR 5 sampling-location annual-average PFAS benchmark results vary with the racial, ethnic, socioeconomic, and rural composition of populations served, after adjustment for water-system and regional characteristics. The design is a national cross-sectional ecological analysis.

Race and ethnicity are treated as markers of social and structural conditions, not biological susceptibility. An association can identify an inequitable monitoring pattern, but this design cannot establish the pathway that produced it.

02

Authoritative sources and frozen release

Every public number is generated from one versioned analysis source.

  • PFAS monitoring: EPA UCMR 5 occurrence records received through January 15, 2026 and EPA’s January 2026 Average PFAS Results export.
  • System characteristics: EPA SDWIS Federal Reporting Services.
  • Service areas: EPA Public Water System Service Areas version 3, including system-sourced and modeled boundaries.
  • Demographics: 2020–2024 American Community Survey block-group estimates and Census Planning Database urban/rural fields.
  • ZIP lookup only: EPA’s PWS-reported UCMR service-ZIP associations.
Release lock. Manuscript tables and website records are generated from release ucmr5-2026-01-15-analysis-v0.2. They are not calculated independently in the browser. Input and artifact hashes are recorded so later refreshes cannot silently alter the reported results.

EPA described the January 2026 data as approximately 95% of expected UCMR 5 results and planned a final release for fall 2026. The manuscript must identify this as an interim federal release and perform one controlled refresh before submission if the final release is available.

03

Cohort construction and sampling design

The primary denominator follows the federal monitoring design.

10,289systems in EPA’s average-results export
8,936active CWSs with complete monitoring
8,701eligible demographic records
8,069systems in the primary inferential cohort

UCMR 5 attempted to monitor all eligible systems serving at least 3,300 people and selected 800 smaller systems through a population-weighted, source- and size-stratified design intended to support national occurrence estimates. Public row-level inclusion probabilities or analysis weights were not identified. The primary inferential cohort therefore includes active community water systems serving at least 3,300 people with complete monitoring, an eligible service-area boundary, and sufficient demographic coverage.

The 632 monitored smaller community systems with eligible demographic records are reported separately as an explicitly unweighted sensitivity; they do not represent all small U.S. systems. Nontransient noncommunity systems are excluded from demographic models because residential Census characteristics do not describe school, workplace, hospital, and other nonresidential populations.

Why the old ZIP analysis was rejected. Copying a system’s detections to every reported service ZIP and retaining each ZIP’s maximum omitted nondetection-only ZIPs and repeatedly counted systems. Those operations cannot estimate national prevalence or system-level disparities. ZIPs now route users to potentially relevant systems only; they are not an analytic denominator.
04

Sampling-location outcomes

EPA annual averages replace detection-only maxima.

  1. 1

    Use complete sampling sets

    Annual averages are defined at an EPA sampling location using its complete required monitoring set. Results below the UCMR minimum reporting level are assigned zero for the calculation.

  2. 2

    Reproduce EPA classifications

    The primary source is EPA’s January 2026 Average PFAS Results export. A record-level reconstruction is retained as a validation analysis rather than as a substitute for unresolved replacement-record logic.

  3. 3

    Classify the combined primary outcome

    A system is positive when at least one complete-set location meets an April 2024 individual-compound or qualifying-mixture technical comparison.

ComparisonUnrounded classification cutoffReason
PFOA or PFOS annual average≥0.00405 µg/LReproduces EPA significant-figure classification around 0.004 µg/L
PFHxS, PFNA, or HFPO-DA annual average≥0.015 µg/LReproduces EPA significant-figure classification around 0.010 µg/L
Hazard Index mixture≥1.5 with ≥2 detected componentsReproduces EPA’s January 2026 technical-assistance classification

These cutoffs reconstruct a rounded federal comparison; they are not newly proposed toxicological thresholds. The manuscript and website use “benchmark” or “technical comparison,” never “violation” or “noncompliance.” Compound-specific outcomes are secondary. Sparse or nonconvergent adjusted models are reported descriptively rather than forced into unstable estimates.

Policy timestamp. The April 2024 rule is the fixed scenario used in this release. EPA proposed in May 2026 to rescind provisions specific to PFHxS, PFNA, HFPO-DA, and mixture Hazard Index requirements. As checked August 14, 2026, that proposal was not treated as final. Rule status must be checked again at submission.
05

Service areas and community characteristics

Demographics are estimated for service areas, not assigned from headquarters ZIP codes.

EPA’s water-system-to-2020-Census-block-group crosswalk links each system service area with block groups. The primary aggregation uses residential-building-footprint weights; area weighting is a sensitivity. Demographic records are retained when block-group links with valid core measures cover at least 90% of the crosswalk-estimated population.

The joint model includes service-area percentages Hispanic/Latino, non-Hispanic Black, non-Hispanic American Indian/Alaska Native, below the federal poverty level, and rural. System covariates are log2 population served, source-water category, ownership, and EPA region.

Connecticut’s planning-region transition changed block-group identifiers in current Census products. For Connecticut only, the analysis uses the official 2023 Planning Database to recover geography-aligned 2017–2021 ACS characteristics and 2020 urban/rural counts on the original 2020 identifiers used by EPA. All 56 primary-cohort systems using that fallback are flagged; a latest-vintage-only sensitivity excludes them.

06

Statistical analysis and uncertainty

Prevalence ratios, spatial diagnostics, and prespecified stress tests.

  • Adjusted prevalence ratios are estimated with modified Poisson regression.
  • EPA-region fixed effects address broad regional differences; leave-one-region-out analyses assess influence.
  • Residual spatial autocorrelation is tested using permutation-based Moran’s I at 4, 8, and 12 nearest neighbors.
  • Because the prespecified diagnostic remained positive, primary uncertainty uses a Conley-style spatial heteroskedasticity-and-autocorrelation-consistent sandwich estimator with a 250-km Bartlett kernel.
  • State-clustered variance and 100- and 500-km spatial cutoffs are uncertainty sensitivities.
  • Secondary compound-specific coefficients are subjected to event-count and convergence gates and Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate control.

The spatial amendment was logged after the residual diagnostic rather than presented as prespecified. At eight neighbors, the primary-model Pearson residual Moran’s I was 0.119 (999-permutation two-sided P=.001).

Required sensitivity analyses include area versus building weights, latest-vintage Census data only, all monitored CWSs as an unweighted sample, system-sourced service boundaries only, alternative spatial cutoffs, leave-one-EPA-region-out refits, and size/source strata. A model is not reported when separation or nonconvergence makes the estimate unreliable.

07

Preliminary primary findings

Shown for transparency; not a substitute for peer review.

Of 8,069 primary-cohort systems, 964 (11.9%) met at least one April 2024 technical comparison. In the joint adjusted model, each 10-percentage-point higher Hispanic service-area composition corresponded to a prevalence ratio of 1.15 (95% CI, 1.09–1.22). Estimates for non-Hispanic Black composition (PR, 1.02; 95% CI, 0.93–1.11) and non-Hispanic American Indian/Alaska Native composition (PR, 1.18; 95% CI, 0.91–1.53) were null or imprecise.

Poverty composition (PR, 0.74; 95% CI, 0.64–0.87) and rural composition (PR, 0.94; 95% CI, 0.91–0.97) were inversely associated with the combined benchmark outcome. These directions must be reported as prominently as the positive Hispanic-composition association and investigated—not rationalized away.

The Hispanic-composition estimate was similar across the primary geographic, cohort, uncertainty, regional-influence, and most size/source sensitivities. Stability does not convert an ecological cross-sectional association into a causal or individual-level claim.

08

Role of the website

Connected to the analysis, but not allowed to redefine it.

The website is a secondary implementation of the frozen release. A ZIP search retrieves PWS-reported service associations and then displays deterministic system-level EPA annual-average comparisons. The interface does not choose the manuscript denominator, reclassify outcomes, fill missing data, or generate health-risk scores. An optional generative-AI question-and-answer panel can explain the displayed public-system context on request, but it makes no automatic lookup request, cannot change any value or classification, and must not be used as an individual exposure, compliance, diagnosis, or medical-decision tool.

How to read a lookup result

A ZIP association identifies one or more systems that reported serving that ZIP. It does not prove which system serves a particular address. An above-comparison flag identifies monitoring evidence at a system sampling location; it does not establish a household concentration, personal exposure, or compliance determination.

09

Limitations

The constraints that define the scope of every conclusion.

  • Ecological design: service-area composition cannot be assigned to individuals and cannot establish individual exposure.
  • Cross-sectional timing: associations do not establish causality or historical exposure.
  • Interim release: the January 2026 UCMR 5 file is not the final federal release.
  • Service-area uncertainty: some EPA boundaries are modeled, and demographic interpolation does not eliminate within-area heterogeneity.
  • Small-system sampling: unweighted monitored systems below 3,300 people do not support inference to all small systems or demographic subgroups.
  • Correlated community measures: compositional predictors are interdependent; adjusted coefficients are not isolated causal effects.
  • Benchmark interpretation: technical-assistance classifications based on UCMR monitoring are not regulatory compliance determinations.
  • Population totals: summed reported system populations can double-count people served through wholesale-retail networks.
  • Policy instability: the federal regulatory configuration may change after this release.
10

Governance, validation, and review status

Transparent about what has and has not been independently checked.

Automated tests cover annual-average comparison rules, Hazard Index classification, prevalence-ratio recovery, and false-discovery-rate behavior. The EPA average-results normalization was validated against the January 2026 federal summary counts. Release artifacts carry cryptographic hashes.

The manuscript and analysis have not completed independent peer review. Planned or requested review is not represented as completed review, institutional endorsement, or authorship. Authorship must reflect substantive contribution, accountability, final approval, and the ability to defend the work; family relationship, title, or affiliation is not evidence of contribution.

The remaining high-priority external review targets are below-quantitation handling, mixture construction, service-area interpolation, compositional modeling, spatial inference, and the final policy interpretation. Any resulting method change must be logged and rerun through the same pipeline.
